Does acupuncture hurt?

Back to the blog

12 November 2026 · Liping Jiang

This is the question I am asked most often, and it is a fair one. Needles sound uncomfortable, and nobody wants to be surprised.

Acupuncture needles are much finer than the needles used to take blood or give an injection. Healthdirect, the Australian government health information service, notes that acupuncture does not usually hurt. During a session you may feel a sensation that is sore, numb or full around a point.

What it can feel like

People notice different things, and that is normal. You might feel:

  • a brief pinch or sting as a needle is placed
  • a dull ache, warmth, tingling or a sense of heaviness
  • very little at all at some points

Once the needles are in, many people rest quietly. If a sensation is sharp, strong or worrying, tell me straight away. I can adjust or remove a needle. Your comfort matters more than finishing a point I had planned.

After the session

It is common to feel relaxed, or sometimes a little tired. Mild tenderness, a small amount of bleeding or a bruise can occur at a needle site. Some people feel dizzy or slightly nauseous. These effects are usually short-lived, but please let me know if they happen, and see your general practitioner if you are concerned.

If you feel sleepy, do not drive until you feel better.

Safety, in plain terms

Any procedure that punctures the skin has some risk, including infection. I use sterile, single-use needles and follow infection-control practice to reduce that risk.

Acupuncture is considered suitable for most people when it is provided by a qualified practitioner, but it is not free of side effects. Uncommon but more serious problems, such as injury to a nerve or organ, can occur. A registered acupuncturist is trained to take care around these areas.

Before you book, talk with your general practitioner if you are pregnant, have a bleeding disorder, take blood-thinning medicine, have a lowered immune system, or have another condition you are unsure about. You can also tell me about these things at your consultation so we can decide what is appropriate.

If you would like to ask more before a first visit, you are welcome to book an appointment or contact the clinic.
